# Made to Fit, v2: making it fun

## Why v1 isn't fun

A player who tried v1 for 10 minutes said it's "a repetition of talking to people around the town." In 10 minutes, v1 gives you:
- about six "Who adjusts?" menus;
- about six conversations;
- one mini-game.

Nothing in the town changes where you can see it.

Daniel Cook's terms explain the problem. A *loop* is something you repeat and get better at. An *arc* is content you use up once, like dialogue. v1 is all arcs and no loop.

In every game we studied (Animal Crossing, Stardew, Pokémon, A Short Hike, GTA), talking is a reward or a gate, never the main verb.

## Pillars

1. **Make room.** You physically remake a muted town that was built for an imaginary "Standard Resident." Each fix brings color back to a street and to the people on it.
2. **Notice, ask, make.** The verbs are physical:
   - raise the lantern and tag what pinches;
   - collect people's own words;
   - build the fix and watch it change the street.
3. **Every fix costs someone.** Trade-offs are the puzzle. Listening to the person who loses something changes the design, and wins votes.
4. **The hook, shown.** The game hooks you with the same tools apps use (points, sets that are almost complete, surprise rewards, a streak in Blend). Before you finish, it shows you each one, using your own play data.

## The three clocks

- **Every 30 seconds: notice.** Raise Wren's lantern (L, or the lantern button). What pinches here glows as marks: ♪ sound, ☀ glare, ✱ crowd, ✎ unclear words, ⏱ fixed hours, ↻ unannounced change, ▲ steps.
  - Tag a mark to collect it. It pops, chimes, and fills the street's checklist ("Market Square 2/4").
  - Some marks appear only under conditions (the noon rush, the 5:40 bus). Those are the rare finds.
- **Every 5 minutes: a fix.**
  1. Tag the street's marks.
  2. Hear the people it pinches. Their words become **story cards**.
  3. Sketch the fix at the Fix-It Shed (the zoning puzzle, or a choice of designs that shows who gains and who loses).
  4. Get it approved:
     - **small fixes** Clover builds on the spot, for coins;
     - **big fixes** need Gil's sign-off or a council hearing.
  5. Watch it built. A color wave rolls over the street, residents come back to their own colors and shapes, and the Town Fit meter climbs.
- **Every session: make room before the Lantern Festival.** Town Fit runs from about 10% to as high as you can get it. Count the residents back in their true colors. At the festival you see who can come.

## Collections (the Fit Book)

The Fit Book is the phone's Notebook, rebuilt. Each collection has a log, a reason to exist, and a use:
- **Marks:** what pinches, by street. They open blueprints.
- **People:** each resident's page has four lines (too much, too little, how they say no, what helps). The more you know, the stronger their story cards are at a hearing.
- **Story cards** (their own words) and **insight cards** (real research, with sources). These are your hand at hearings.
- **Fixes built:** each with who it helps and its design principle.
- **Ledger pages:** five hidden pages of the founders' ledger. Together they reveal how the Standard Resident was invented.
- **Pond fish:** a side collection with rare fish that only appear under conditions.

Endowed progress: each checklist starts part-filled. Every log says what's left ("1 page left").

## Hearings (persuasion as play)

This is a council hearing in the style of Ace Attorney's "present the evidence":
- Each council member raises one typed objection:
  - Hollis, TRADITION: "We have always done it this way."
  - Pip, BUSINESS: "Does it bring people through my door?"
  - Clover, FAIRNESS: "Who did you ask about who it costs?"
  - Gil, COST: "Corporate loves the panels."
- You answer with a card from what you've collected:
  - an **insight**: the curb-cut effect against "only a few need it," or "nobody is average" (Daniels, 1952) against tradition;
  - a **story**: Gil's own headaches, Pip's seven dropped trays;
  - a **compromise**: the changed design you agreed with the person who loses something.
- A strong answer turns the vote: a YES stamp lands. A weak answer costs composure. Two of three votes carry the fix.
- Losing is soft. The fix goes on file, and Clover tells you which card you were missing.

The lesson lands because the right card really is the right argument.

## The town reacts

- **Color.** A shader desaturates each street until its fixes land.
- **People.** Residents start partly "drifted" toward the Standard Resident's grey shape (the same effect that fades you when you push through). As their places fit them, they come back as themselves.
- **Curb-cut moments.** When a fix helps people it wasn't built for, they show up: a stroller on the ramp, Pip's cart. A popup names the effect.

## Blend and the reveal

Blend, Rue's app, offers to make you fit instead: points for pushing through, a streak, surprise bonuses, likes from neighbors. Its points come from the same moments that grey you.

Every hook is logged when it fires:
- Blend's hooks: streak, variable bonus, likes;
- the game's own hooks: XP pops, "1 left!" prompts, sets almost complete, open loops at the end of each day.

After the festival, a "How this game kept you playing" page reveals them:
- each hook, with your own count ("You opened the Fit Book 31 times; you chased two '1 left' sets");
- the research behind it, with sources;
- where you meet it in real life.

Then you choose: keep the hooks on, or play the epilogue and later visits in quiet mode.

What stays out: real money, notifications, timers on real time, and anything that punishes you for leaving. The research on dark patterns puts the line at hiding costs and working against the player.

## The days

Each day lasts about 12 to 15 minutes.

**Monday: notice.**
1. Your room is in full color. Step out, and the town is muted.
2. Pick up Wren's lantern from the sill and raise it on Home Row: two marks.
3. Bea comes out: three short lines, then her story card.
4. Clover is fixing Bea's gate. She builds your porch lantern (teal means come in, amber means not now) on the spot.
5. The first color wave rolls over Home Row, Bea turns back to her own colors, and Town Fit and your level both go up. Target: under 4 minutes after the creator.
6. Then the Works (sort mail for coins; marks inside), the streets, the Shed as your hub, and Blend through Rue.
7. Open loops at the end of the day: streets with marks left, Clover's "found something in the loft."

**Tuesday: ask.**
- The ledger hunt (five pages, seen with the lantern), then the reveal. First, guess how many residents match the Standard. Then the answer: none.
- The weekly sync (stamping permits).
- Big fixes: sketch them, gather backers, listen to the person each one costs.
- The hearing at 6 PM.

**Wednesday: make room.**
- Fixes in use, as small scenes at their own hours.
- Painting lanterns for people you know.
- Rue's choice about Blend.
- The festival: the town in color, and residents in their true colors join you.
- The hook reveal.
- The ending.

The later visits (ten and twenty years on) stay, carried over from what you built.

## Feel

- Every verb gets juice: pops, chimes, particles, a small camera push, and squash on residents when they're happy.
- Hold Shift to jog. A bike unlocks at level 3.
- "Who adjusts?" stays as the thesis. It comes up the first time a place pinches you on a given day, and never more than once a minute. Afterwards, a small chip offers the quick answers.
- Conversations: at most three lines a beat. The "how did that go?" reflection runs twice, not every time.

## Ethics and accuracy (unchanged)

- No simulating impairment on the player's senses. The lantern shows the place's properties (where the sound comes from), not a distorted view.
- Residents speak in their own words.
- Even-handed.
- Every number lives in a sourced insight card.

## Revised after review (this is the build plan)

Most players arrive from a resume link and play 5 to 15 minutes. So the first day is a 12-minute slice that holds the whole arc. Days 2 and 3, and the later visits, become "keep playing."

**The slice**

0. **Bus and creator.**
   - Room setup becomes six quick two-picture taps.
   - The muted town is shown with the grey Standard statue in the shot, so the grey has a cause.
1. **Home Row, wave by about 3:00.**
   - Raise the lantern and tag two marks. Bea pops out with two lines.
   - *You* hang your porch lantern, choosing teal (come in) or amber (not now). The wave rolls out from your door, and Bea comes back to her colors.
   - The lantern stays a live toggle: on teal, people drop by; on amber, they nod and pass.
2. **Market Square, wave by about 6:00.**
   - Tag the two speakers; the mark ducks the street audio for two seconds as a preview.
   - Ask Moss. Pick between two designs, one of which costs Pomeroy his loud side.
   - Listen to Pomeroy, then build. The wave follows.
   - Blend is offered here.
3. **Ramp hearing, by about 10:00.**
   - Bea's cart meets the Works steps; the ▲ mark draws the 140 m detour.
   - A 90-second hearing follows: contradictions, one free "Ask why," single-use cards, consent before using someone's story, and a witness from the gallery.
   - The gavel goes straight to the build, then a stroller and Pip's cart use the ramp (curb-cut).
4. **Lantern night at dusk.**
   - The guests you made room for arrive, each bringing something.
   - Tune the lantern to the Standard Resident: the town shows zero marks, because nobody is the Standard (Daniels, 1952).
   - Sort today's rewards into "pointed me at something real" and "pointed me at the hook."
   - The postcard (fit card). Then "Keep playing: Day 2."

**Changes to the plan above**

- **Progress:** a guest list (who can come to lantern night, and later the festival) instead of Town Fit %, XP and levels. The points live in Blend, which is the mirror.
- **Hooks are shown from minute one:** a small hook glyph sits beside every reward pop, and a Hook Log explains each one.
- **Blend's temptation:** it skips "Who adjusts?" cards for you, but your lantern dims and marks fade. It counts uses in a row, not days.
- **Residents grey only in color and expression,** and only where a place pinches them. Never their bodies, carts or aids. Everyone is in full color at home.
- **Calm feedback is available from the start,** in settings and the comfort check.
- **A toy verb:** your comfort move on a key. In restored streets it plays in key, and Wren hums back.
- **Movement:** easing, a lean, dust puffs, a hop, and Shift to jog.
- **"Who adjusts?"** shows once as a full card, then as a chip that doesn't pause the game.
